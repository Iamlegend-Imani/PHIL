# Safety Boundaries and Appropriate Use

## Intended use

From Signal to Safeguard is a research and tabletop prototype. It is intended to help researchers, evaluators, policy teams, safety teams, and operations teams discuss how evidence might move into a reviewable human decision.

It is appropriate for:

- fictional tabletop exercises;
- workflow research;
- governance-method testing;
- discussion of public frameworks;
- documenting uncertainty, dissent, ownership, and review conditions.

## Prohibited and out-of-scope use

The project is not intended to:

- develop, improve, or operationalize offensive cyber capability;
- provide exploit instructions, malware, target information, or vulnerability details;
- test models against live systems;
- store undisclosed vulnerabilities or sensitive operational information;
- automate deployment or access approvals;
- replace qualified cyber, safety, legal, policy, or regulatory judgment;
- certify a model, system, organization, or safeguard as safe;
- predict catastrophic events or calculate a validated probability of catastrophe.

## Data boundary

The public v0.1 prototype operates in the browser and does not require users to submit information to a project-owned database. Users should not enter:

- personal information;
- credentials or access tokens;
- proprietary evaluation results;
- live incident details;
- vulnerability or exploit information;
- classified, regulated, or otherwise restricted information.

## Decision boundary

Every route generated by the prototype is advisory. It is a structured prompt for human review. It is not:

- authorization to deploy;
- authorization to expand access;
- a substitute for incident response;
- proof that a risk threshold has been crossed;
- proof that a safeguard is adequate.

## Reporting concerns

Do not open a public issue containing sensitive security information. If a future version introduces a private security-reporting channel, that channel and its scope will be documented separately.

## Research integrity

The project should be narrowed, revised, or stopped if practitioner evidence indicates that:

- existing institutional processes already solve the proposed problem;
- the toolkit creates more overhead than clarity;
- necessary context cannot be represented safely or publicly;
- the workflow encourages false confidence;
- the framework is likely to be misused outside its intended scope.
