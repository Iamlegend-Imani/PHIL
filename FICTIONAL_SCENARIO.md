# Fictional Patient Scenario

## Disclosure

Every person, event, result, date, and quotation in this scenario is fictional. The scenario exists only to demonstrate the PHIL workflow. It is not medical advice and is not intended to model a diagnosis.

## Persona

**Name:** Maya Carter  
**Age:** 42  
**Location:** Boston, Massachusetts  
**Visit type:** Scheduled follow-up  
**Primary concern:** Persistent fatigue with changes in sleep consistency  
**Practical capacity:** Two changes at a time  
**Budget context:** Up to $75 per month in recurring out-of-pocket costs

## In Maya's words

> I want to understand what is worth discussing first, without losing the reality of my life.

## Why Maya is preparing

Maya has a follow-up appointment after several months of intermittent fatigue. She has information in several places, but she does not feel that any single source tells the story:

- her memory of when the fatigue changed
- a current medication and supplement list
- two fictional lab summaries from prior visits
- a weekly wearable summary showing less consistent sleep timing
- a work schedule that recently added late shifts
- notes about what she has already tried

Maya is not asking PHIL to tell her what is wrong. She wants to prepare a coherent account, verify the details, and decide what questions matter most before the visit.

## Fictional inputs

### Patient story

- Fatigue was intermittent at the start of the year and became more disruptive by spring.
- Late work shifts increased from roughly one to three nights most weeks.
- Maya describes difficulty keeping sleep and wake times consistent.
- She can realistically manage no more than two new actions at once.
- She wants any recurring out-of-pocket step to stay within $75 per month.

### Medication and supplement list

- One fictional prescribed medication appears on a patient-maintained list.
- One over-the-counter supplement was added in April after Maya read online reviews.
- The supplement name and dose are intentionally not used to invite interpretation.
- The entire list is marked “needs verification” until reconciled against a trusted source with a qualified professional.

### Mock record summary

- A routine fictional panel was completed in January.
- A second fictional panel was added in May.
- PHIL displays the existence and timing of the documents but does not interpret values.

### Mock wearable summary

- Weekly sleep-duration and schedule-consistency ranges are displayed.
- March through May show greater variation than January.
- The interface states that this timing may be useful to discuss but does not establish cause.

## Maya's priorities

1. Have enough energy for daily life, especially through late work shifts.
2. Understand which changes are useful to discuss first.
3. Choose a realistic next step that fits her schedule and budget.

## Questions Maya selects

- Which changes in my symptoms or routine are most useful to investigate first?
- Could we reconcile my medication and supplement list together?
- Do the changes across my prior results alter what you would want to review?
- What is the smallest realistic next step given my schedule and budget?

## Deliberate ambiguity

The scenario contains several events that occur near one another. PHIL does not resolve whether any one of them explains the fatigue. That ambiguity is the point of the safety design.

The timeline exists to help Maya and a qualified professional ask better questions. It does not establish causation, diagnosis, or treatment.

## Human-review moment

Before the brief can be approved, the prototype requires verification of identity, medication source, patient priorities, uncertainty, and clinical claim language.

The medication list remains visibly pending. This demonstrates that a brief can be useful while still showing what has not yet been verified.
