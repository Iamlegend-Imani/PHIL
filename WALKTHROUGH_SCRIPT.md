# 90-Second Walkthrough

## Voiceover script

**Target length:** 90 seconds  
**Tone:** Calm, direct, curious, and grounded  
**Status:** Ready to record

### 0:00 to 0:12

Your health is not fragmented. Your health data is.

I built PHIL, a Personal Health Intelligence Layer, to explore a simple question: what changes when health technology starts with the whole person, not just the easiest data point?

### 0:12 to 0:27

Meet Maya, a fictional patient preparing for a follow-up visit. Her symptoms, medication list, prior documents, sleep patterns, work schedule, budget, and goals live in different places. PHIL brings that context into one patient-controlled view.

### 0:27 to 0:41

The Context Map connects biology, behavior, care, and lived reality. Every input keeps its source and verification state. The system does not claim these connections are causal. It makes them visible so people can ask better questions.

### 0:41 to 0:54

The Health Timeline places events in sequence, while a clear uncertainty boundary prevents timing from becoming diagnosis. Maya can see what changed without software pretending to know why.

### 0:54 to 1:08

Next, Maya chooses the questions that belong in her Visit Preparation Brief. Her priorities, practical capacity, and budget remain part of the story because a theoretically perfect next step is not useful if it cannot fit her life.

### 1:08 to 1:22

Before the brief can be shared, a human verifies identity, sources, patient intent, uncertainty, and the absence of unsupported clinical claims. Incomplete review blocks approval.

### 1:22 to 1:30

PHIL does not diagnose or prescribe. It organizes. The patient owns the story. The professional owns clinical judgment. Capability can be automated. Accountability cannot.

## Recording notes

- Record at 1920 by 1080 or 1440 by 900.
- Keep cursor movement slow and intentional.
- Do not show any real health information or browser notifications.
- Use the fictional-data label in at least one frame.
- Leave the final safety statement on screen long enough to read.
- Use captions even if voiceover is included.
