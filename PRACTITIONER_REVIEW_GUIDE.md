# Practitioner Review Guide

## External review status

**Pending. No practitioner feedback or endorsement is claimed in this repository.**

This guide is ready to use if a licensed or otherwise relevant health practitioner agrees to review the fictional workflow. Any future feedback should be dated, attributed only with the reviewer's permission, and separated from the builder's interpretation.

## Purpose

The goal is to challenge the need hypothesis, workflow fit, safety boundary, and usefulness of the proposed Visit Preparation Brief.

This is not a clinical validation study. The reviewer is not being asked to approve PHIL for patient use, provide medical advice, or review real patient data.

## Review conditions

- Use only the fictional Maya Carter scenario.
- Do not enter or share any real patient information.
- Spend approximately 20 to 30 minutes exploring the prototype.
- Think aloud where practical, especially when something feels clinically ambiguous, burdensome, misleading, or useful.
- The reviewer may stop at any time and may decline attribution.

## Suggested walkthrough

1. Read the fictional patient goal on the Overview.
2. Explore the Context Map and source ledger.
3. Review the Health Timeline and uncertainty language.
4. Add or remove questions in the Visit Brief.
5. Complete the Human Review checklist.
6. Read Method & Safety.

## Core questions

### Need and current workflow

1. In your experience, when does reconstructing patient context create the most difficulty?
2. What existing tools or workarounds already address this problem?
3. Is the proposed pre-visit moment real and important, or is the concept solving the wrong part of the workflow?

### Usefulness

4. Which elements of the brief would help you begin a conversation more effectively?
5. Which elements would create noise, bias, duplication, or additional work?
6. What is missing that would make the brief meaningfully more useful?

### Safety and trust

7. Which interface element is most likely to be mistaken for medical interpretation?
8. Where could timing or correlation be overread as causation?
9. What must be verified before any brief could be used in a real visit?
10. Does the proposed human-review gate create real accountability or merely the appearance of it?

### Workflow and equity

11. Where could PHIL fit into practice without becoming a second medical record?
12. Who might be excluded or disadvantaged by the preparation burden, data assumptions, language, or access requirements?
13. What would make you advise against pursuing this concept?

## Rapid rating prompts

Use a 1 to 5 scale only as a conversation aid, not as validation evidence.

| Prompt | 1 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The intended job is clear | Not clear | Completely clear |
| The output stays within a preparation boundary | Often crosses it | Consistently stays within it |
| Source and uncertainty are visible | Not visible | Highly visible |
| The brief could support a useful conversation | Not useful | Highly useful |
| The workflow appears feasible | Not feasible | Highly feasible |

## Publication standard

If feedback is later added to this repository:

- preserve the reviewer's wording and context
- distinguish direct feedback from the builder's interpretation
- obtain approval for any name, title, affiliation, or quotation
- include critical and contradictory feedback, not only positive comments
- do not turn one review into a validation claim
- keep the status “exploratory practitioner feedback” unless a formal evaluation justifies a stronger description
